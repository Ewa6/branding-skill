# Rebranding: establish what changes before designing it

Use this path when the owner wants to refresh or change an existing brand. Merely finishing an incomplete identity uses the continuation path. Rebranding can be a small visual evolution or a change in positioning, audience, name and identity; don't assume either extreme.

Use [the rebranding-plan template](../assets/rebranding-plan.md) for the audit, component scope and transition. Keep it concise for a small refresh.

## 1. Understand the reason and recover the baseline

Inspect the current identity in real applications: logo, name, positioning, copy, palette, type, graphics and priority channels. Read existing decisions and any actual audience/business evidence. Record a version, commit, dated source links or permitted snapshot so the original remains recoverable. Do not duplicate a whole repository or private customer material into the brand package.

Establish:
- What changed in the product, audience or ambition?
- What specifically is not working, and where is it visible?
- What does the owner still value, and what do they want to leave behind?
- What would a better result enable or feel like?
- Which channels and assets are actually in scope?

Infer supported answers and ask only material gaps. A vague “looks dated” can justify a visual comparison; it is not evidence that customers want a new name. Distinguish owner preference, observed usability/legibility problems, business evidence and hypotheses. Do not invent brand recognition, customer reactions or market research.

## 2. Agree the depth and component scope

Recommend a proportionate route using the reason and evidence:
- **Visual refresh:** improve execution while keeping agreed foundations such as name and positioning.
- **Identity evolution:** materially change selected visual/verbal elements while retaining specific recognition cues.
- **Strategic rebrand:** reconsider positioning, audience or promise and derive the needed identity changes; naming is included only when opened.

These are discussion aids, not fixed service packages. A single-logo redesign need not become a positioning project.

For each relevant component choose **retain, evolve, replace, retire or unresolved**, with reason and owner/delegated decision source. Separate constraints (a legally required name, stock size, an approved unchangeable mark) from preferences that may be reconsidered. The current instruction can reopen an earlier approved choice.

If scope is delegated or already explicit, record it and proceed. Otherwise present the concrete recommended scope and resolve the choice that affects dependent work. Do not generate a wholesale replacement merely because the word “rebrand” appears.

## 3. Compare the current brand with purposeful changes

Enter the shared workflow at the earliest affected phase:
- Changed audience/benefit/difference: revisit [brief and positioning](brief.md), then the affected voice and visuals.
- Renaming in scope: use [naming](naming.md), including current checks.
- Visual change only: retain positioning/name/voice unless specifically opened; proceed to [visual direction](visual-direction.md) and relevant production references.

Use identical realistic applications and content to compare baseline and proposed routes. Explain how each change addresses the brief, and what recognition or useful familiarity it preserves or sacrifices. An explicit fresh start can be right; preserving everything is not the goal.

Keep retained sources authoritative. Do not call a redrawn mark faithful when it is a replacement. Conversely, the lack of an editable old logo should not block an authorized new logo. Preserve product behavior, privacy and physical constraints unless changing them is separately in scope.

Use the [challenger](team.md) to test both unnecessary novelty and unhelpful attachment to the old identity. AI critique remains a recommendation, not customer evidence or owner approval.

## 4. Produce the new system and map the transition

Finish the applicable shared deliverables: logo, voice, book, tokens/type and assets/applications. Keep the original baseline recoverable and new proposals in a separate working location until their intended status is established. A new decision should not rewrite old evidence or silently replace the live product.

Map the affected assets/touchpoints to **reuse, update, replace or retire**, including:
- Current source and replacement source.
- Design status, implementation/activation status and dependencies.
- Who performs the change and the intended order or rollout approach.
- Checks that will demonstrate the change, and how to recover if implementation fails.

Include only relevant touchpoints; don't create a website/domain migration for a palette refresh. If naming changes, identify affected URLs, email, handles, downloads and printed stock where they exist. Record transition copy or an announcement as a draft only when useful/in scope; never send it automatically.

Do not infer that everything should switch at once or that mixed old/new material is automatically an error. Agree a suitable phased or single release; label intentional coexistence. Avoid marking an old production asset retired merely because its replacement is designed.

## 5. Delivery and activation are different

A completed design package can be ready while rollout remains pending. The [delivery index](delivery.md) must say which identity is currently live, which designs are approved/staged, what remains unresolved and which implementation actions have actually occurred.

Implementation, publishing, redirect changes, renaming external accounts and contacting customers require the appropriate authorization. If those actions are already authorized, carry them out within scope and verify the result; don't ask again as a routine final gate.

For a design-only request, deliver the transition plan and handoff without treating pending implementation as unfinished design. For an authorized implementation request, don't claim the rebrand is live until affected touchpoints and deployment have been verified.
