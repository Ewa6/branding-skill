# Verbal identity and usable copy

Use [the voice template](../assets/tone-of-voice.md), adapted to the product rather than copying NieZamulaj's encouragement.

Define audience needs, address/register, a few distinctive voice traits with boundaries, and the brand's central promise. Connect positioning to messaging pillars with supporting evidence. Separate a tagline candidate from an accepted tagline.

Show voice changing with the situation while identity stays recognizable:
- Public headline and short product description.
- First use and ordinary action.
- Success/progress where relevant.
- Empty state, uncertainty, failure and recovery.
- Sensitive contexts relevant to the product.

Provide do/don't rewrites, vocabulary/glossary, spelling/case, punctuation, numbers, button naming and localization rules. Use native phrasing in each supported language; mark working translations. Preserve explicit owner preferences such as removing artificial punctuation. A two-line heading does not require full stops after fragments.

For each quiet/error-state recipe state trigger, truthful product state, message, primary action and optional secondary action. Do not claim data is safe, offline works or an action is reversible unless verified. A proposed UI is not an implemented capability.

Review copy in actual layouts and with long text. Distinguish full source text from an explicitly authored short print/display title; don't silently truncate meaning. Use fictional examples where public/privacy rules require them.

The final voice guide should stand alone for someone writing future copy. Keep a Markdown source and a polished shareable form (in the brand book and a separate PDF if useful/requested). Do not require a second language or a “bad day” story for a brand with no such need.
