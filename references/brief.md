# Brief and positioning

Use [the brief template](../assets/brief.md). Read project artifacts and the current decisions first.

Capture enough to make design choices:
- Product: what it does, for whom, in what real situation.
- Need and benefit: the audience's problem and the specific value the product can credibly offer.
- Difference: why someone would choose it over their current alternative; distinguish evidence from aspiration.
- Positioning: a short internal sentence linking audience, category, benefit and difference.
- Personality and desired feeling, with relevant references and exclusions.
- Channels and deliverables, languages, current name/logo, time and budget.
- Practical constraints such as label size, printer, packaging dimensions, smallest screen or legibility.
- Public/private boundaries and existing assets with approval status.

Do not write a business plan as a prerequisite to a small identity. A short positioning paragraph and a few messaging pillars usually suffice. Avoid inventing market research, superiority claims or audience findings.

Inspect earlier product iterations for durable requirements and abandoned mechanics. Preserve the distinction between product behavior and how the brand communicates it.

Show a concise synthesis and surface only choices that affect the next work. If the owner already authorized the direction, use it and proceed. Log material corrections.
